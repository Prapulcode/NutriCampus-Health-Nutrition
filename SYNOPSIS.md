### 1. Project Title
**Health and Nutrition Awareness Web Application for College Students**

---

### 2. Problem Statement
College students may have difficulty finding simple, reliable, and easy-to-understand information about balanced diets, hydration, healthy habits, and basic nutrition. Busy schedules, irregular meals, and dependence on unverified online advice can make it harder to develop healthy routines. A simple awareness website can present general educational information and interactive tools in one accessible place. The application will provide awareness only and will not diagnose conditions or replace advice from qualified healthcare professionals.

---

### 3. Target Community / Beneficiaries
The primary beneficiaries are college students, especially undergraduate students who want to learn basic nutrition and develop healthier daily habits. Secondary beneficiaries may include student clubs and college staff who conduct health-awareness activities. The initial evaluation will involve a small volunteer group of students from the college.

---

### 4. Objectives
- To provide clear, student-friendly educational content about balanced meals, food groups, hydration, and healthy daily habits.
- To offer a simple BMI calculator for general awareness, with an explanation that BMI is only a screening measure and is not a diagnosis.
- To provide a water-intake tracker and a short nutrition-awareness quiz.
- To make the information accessible through a responsive interface on phones and computers.
- To collect basic usability feedback and assess whether the website is easy to understand and navigate.

---

### 5. Proposed Solution / Application Details
The proposed solution is a responsive web application titled “Health and Nutrition Awareness.” Its home page will introduce the purpose of the project and link to the main modules. The nutrition section will explain food groups and give general examples of balanced meals using familiar foods. A BMI tool will calculate BMI from user-entered height and weight and display a neutral educational explanation; it will not provide a medical diagnosis. A water tracker will let users record cups or millilitres consumed during the day, and a quiz will test basic nutrition knowledge with feedback after each answer. A healthy-habits page will present simple, non-medical reminders. The first version will avoid storing sensitive personal health data and will use educational content reviewed for accuracy.

---

### 6. Proposed Methodology
1. Identify common nutrition-awareness questions among college students through informal discussion or a short voluntary questionnaire.
2. Define the website requirements and prepare page layouts and navigation using simple wireframes.
3. Gather and cross-check general educational information from credible public-health sources.
4. Develop the front end using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript; implement optional server-side functionality using Python Flask and SQLite if needed.
5. Implement and test the nutrition pages, BMI calculation, water tracker, and quiz using sample inputs.
6. Ask a small group of volunteer students to try the website, gather usability feedback, fix issues, and document the results.

---

### 7. Hardware and Software Requirements
- **Hardware Requirements:** Laptop or desktop computer for development; smartphone or computer with a web browser for testing; internet access for research and deployment if required.
- **Software Requirements:** Windows or Ubuntu; Visual Studio Code or another code editor; HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript; Python 3 and Flask if a backend is used; SQLite if data storage is required; Google Chrome or Mozilla Firefox for testing; Git/GitHub for version control (optional).

---

### 8. Expected Outcomes
- A working, mobile-friendly health and nutrition awareness website with educational pages and interactive tools.
- Improved access to basic, understandable nutrition and healthy-habit information for participating students.
- A completed BMI calculator, daily water tracker, and nutrition quiz that work with test inputs.
- Feedback from student testers and a short report describing usability, issues found, and improvements made.
- Project documentation, screenshots, and a demonstration of the application. The project will measure awareness and usability feedback, not claim to improve health outcomes without evidence.
